> I'd planned to be more on top of my blogging, but I've been caught in a frenzy of life and due dates. So to make amends, I've decided to attempt a daily-note blog stream habit. Both as a way to keep some accountability and to gather my (increasingly scattered) thoughts.
>
> These notes will likely incorporate some more personal ruminations and will almost certainly be significantly less polished than the norm. Feel free to skip them! When I get some time, I'll probably siphon them away into a separate section of my blog and RSS feed to not clutter up the main feed.

It's almost the half-way mark in Michaemas term and the weather's starting to turn grim. Sunset is frighteningly early and the nights are dark and full of ~~terrors~~ study. At least the leaves are in free-fall, making for quite the beautiful sight here in [Leckhampton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leckhampton,_Corpus_Christi_College,_Cambridge). I bought a [second-hand DSLR lens](https://www.sony.co.uk/lenses/products/sel2860) from [MPB](https://www.mpb.com/en-uk) for my partner's camera and we've managed to get some good shots of the squirrels here. I know nothing about photography, but I might start attaching some images to my CDN and linking them here.

Last month we watched the [Secrets of Squirrels](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt32832464/) and learned that, despite being adorable, the [grey squirrels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_gray_squirrel) in the UK are an invasive species that are displacing their smaller cousin, the [red squirrel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_squirrel).

Study remains, as ever, an absolute joy. But the work has turned into a series of back-to-back assignments and it's harder to find patches to sit down and take notes, read textbooks, and cogitate.

I'm currently reading through [_Large Language Diffusion Models_ by Nie et al. (2025)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.09992) for an assignment for [L173 Probabilistic Machine Learning](https://www.cl.cam.ac.uk/teaching/2627/L173/). Diffusion models are pretty interesting generative models, and I actually had the good fortune to attend NeurIPS 2025 last year where I watched the [poster session](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2025/loc/san-diego/poster/118608) by the LLaDA folks.

Yesterday I had a bit of an existential crisis about the role of AI in academia and what forging a career in a "post-agentic" world looks like. It's been a bit harder than I anticipated adjusting to study in the AI-era: something that I want to shape up into a more detailed personal reflection when I get a chance. I also haven't talked about this here before, but it's a constant battle against impostor syndrome: especially after having taken a break from study since 2022.

I'm still trying to work out what my MPhil thesis will be focusing on. I've got some ideas that I'm excited about, but I don't know how feasible they are yet. In the meantime, as a moment of gratitude, thank you to: [Tobias Grosser](https://grosser.science), [Alexander Viand](https://pps-lab.com/people/alexanderviand/), [Ceren Kocaoğullar](https://www.cl.cam.ac.uk/~ck596/), [Martin Kleppmann](https://martin.kleppmann.com), [Daniel Hugenroth](https://www.danielhugenroth.com), [Jeremy Kun](https://www.jeremykun.com) and [Jon Sterling](https://www.jonmsterling.com/index/) for their time, patience, and advice.

Until tomorrow.
