### Assorted Readings

> I thought it might be nice to jot down some thoughts on recent pieces that I've read.
> I would like to come up with a more systematic way of recording the content I come across, and making my thoughts on it public. But, for now, the daily-note will suffice.

- [AI Doesn’t Have to Go Rogue to Break the World](https://www.schneier.com/blog/archives/2026/11/ai-doesnt-have-to-go-rogue-to-break-the-world.html) on the [Schneier on Security](https://www.schneier.com) blog. I thought this was a superbly well-reasoned post on the _practical_ concerns re AI systems.
- I was surprised to see Numberphile recently put out a video on [A Two-Button Calculator That Does Everything](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LxVW3b8jVdE). It's based on the [All elementary functions from a single binary operator](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.21852v2) paper that I happened to read-through the other week and discussed with [Challenger Mishra](https://www.cst.cam.ac.uk/people/cm2099). I thought the core of it was a nice expression for a "universal" binary operator, but the paper does overstate the finding and brushes over some handling of complex values.
- [2023 Wheeler Lecture - 'A Taste of Verse'](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xNaaP8BKr7Y) by [Simon Peyton Jones](https://simon.peytonjones.org). [Verse](https://verselang.github.io/book/) is an interesting language: I'm not aware of many other functional-logic paradigm blending programming languages. One thing I've not quite understood is what exactly it offers for the world of game programming that other languages don't. There are some intriguing constructs in the language (e.g. "choice"), but I'd love to see some snippets to see how it is applied practically and what painpoints it solves.
