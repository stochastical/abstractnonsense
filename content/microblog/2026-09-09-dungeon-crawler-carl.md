title: Dungeon Crawler Carl by Matt Dinniman
date: 2026-09-09
tags: [books, reading]
---

This is the kind of book that’s normally squarely outside my wheelhouse. It was my first introduction to the genre of “[LitRPG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LitRPG)” (literary role-playing game) and follows a character, Carl, alongside his cat-panion, through a series of increasingly adverserial [dungeon crawl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dungeon_crawl) scenarios.

To be frank, it’s a *completely* ludicrous book: it’s over-the-top fantastical gore mixed with catty humour. I came across it whilst browsing [Hardcover](https://hardcover.app/) (a superb Goodreads replacement[^1]), where it topped the “most read” lists for a few months running. It was also nominated for the [2026 Arthur C. Clarke Award](https://web.archive.org/web/20260819024222/http://www.sfadb.com/Arthur_C_Clarke_Award_2026). 

However, beneath all the macabre violence is a surpisingly astute reflection on the trials and tribulations of being human amidst the harshes of conditions. It’s undeniably entertaining and a pretty pointed lampoon of "brainrot" culture and the political climate. Here, even the rules of traditional sci-fi and fantasy dissipate away in a blaze of frenetic creative energy. All in all, I can’t deny it was a fun read, but I can’t see myself consuming any more in the series or genre.

[^1]: You should also check out [Emma Goto](https://www.emgoto.com/)’s blog. She’s been doing superb work on the Hardcover UI and is a voracious reader.
